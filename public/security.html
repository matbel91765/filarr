How Filarr Web protects your data

This page states exactly what the web app guarantees — and what it cannot. No superlatives: the threat model, as it is.

What is true everywhere

Your files and notes are encrypted (AES-256-GCM) with a key derived from your password, before they leave your device. Our servers store and carry ciphertext only: we cannot read your content, cannot decrypt it for a third party, and cannot recover it if you lose both your password and your recovery phrase.

Two clients, two levels of trust

Desktop appWeb app
The code you run Installed once, inspectable, updated explicitly Served by our servers on every load
Key at rest Sealed in the system keychain (safeStorage) Nothing by default; optionally sealed under a non-extractable browser session key (14 days)
The honest consequence Verifiable zero-knowledge: even our servers, fully compromised, cannot read your files Encrypted in your browser: a compromised server cannot read your stored data, but could serve modified code to a future session. That is the structural limit of every web application — ours and our peers' alike.

Put plainly: the web app gives you end-to-end encryption with the convenience of a browser; the desktop app additionally gives you an execution base we cannot alter after the fact. For the strongest posture, use the desktop app day to day and the web as a secondary access point.

What the web app does to reduce that risk

  • Strict Content-Security-Policy: no third-party scripts, no CDN, no external origin — fonts and code served exclusively from app.filarr.com, API pinned to api.filarr.com.
  • No key in the clear at rest: the encryption key lives in session memory; the "Stay unlocked" option seals it under a non-extractable key managed by the browser — stealing local storage is not enough to obtain it.
  • Your password is never sent for decryption: the key is derived locally (PBKDF2-SHA-512, 600,000 iterations).
  • Two server relays, two SEPARATE agreements, each refused by default and each with its own switch in Settings → Privacy → Security. Accepting one never turns on the other.
    • Link previews (⚡ fill from a link, titles of pasted links, bookmark previews): only the page URL transits.
    • Source lookups (⚡ button of inline databases when a books/anime/TV/movies source is selected): only the few words searched transit, plus your TMDB key if you have set one.
    In both cases your note content never transits, and nothing going through these relays is logged or kept.
  • Session revoked at the first doubt: signing out purges the key and the session.

What we do not promise

We do not promise that a web app equals an installed client — that claim is false everywhere. We do not promise to recover your data without your secrets — that is the price of real encryption. And we never claim "zero-knowledge" for the web without this page next to it.
